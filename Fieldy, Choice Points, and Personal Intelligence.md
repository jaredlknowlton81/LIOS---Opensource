Fieldy, Choice Points, Personal Intelligence, and the Planetary Mesh

Core Idea

The goal isn’t simply to record my life.

The goal is to capture enough real-life experience to identify recurring patterns at moments where choices matter, learn from those patterns, and eventually contribute validated, anonymized patterns to a larger intelligence network.

The system begins with one person.

It can eventually extend to:

Individual → Relationships → Teams → Communities → Organizations → Cities → Planetary Mesh

The fundamental principle is:

Learn locally, protect individually, validate collectively, and share only what can safely become useful collective knowledge.

  

The Personal Experiment

The first experiment is deliberately small.

30-Day Communication Choice-Point Experiment

Question:

Why do some ordinary conversations unexpectedly become tense?

Capture relevant experiences and examine:

- What happened immediately before tension appeared?
- What did I say or do?
- What did the other person do?
- What did I assume they meant?
- What was actually observable?
- What choices were available to me?
- What choice did I make?
- What happened afterward?
- Have I experienced this pattern before?
- Did an alternative response produce a different outcome?

The objective is not to prove that I am right or wrong.

It is to develop a better model of what is actually happening.

  

The Core Learning Loop

Experience → Capture → Context → Pattern → Choice Point → Reflection → Experiment → Feedback → New Experience

The system continuously learns from the loop.

A failed experiment is not failure.

It is information.

A successful experiment is not automatically a universal rule.

It is a hypothesis that may be worth testing elsewhere.

  

Fieldy as the Experience Layer

A wearable such as Fieldy becomes the interface between lived experience and the intelligence system.

It can capture:

- Conversations
- Thoughts
- Decisions
- Context
- Environmental signals
- Reflections
- Outcomes

But capture is only the beginning.

The objective is not to create an infinite recording archive.

The objective is to transform experience into structured, useful, and appropriately protected knowledge.

  

Privacy and Consent Layer

Privacy and consent are architectural components, not optional features.

The system should establish a clear consent state before information enters the processing pipeline.

Recording States

🔴 Private

- Do not record.
- Do not process.

🟡 Personal

- Capture only the individual’s own reflections where appropriate.

🟢 Consented

- Participants have agreed to recording and processing.

⏸️ Paused

- Temporarily stop capture.

🗑️ Delete

- Remove the relevant experience according to the user’s retention policy.

The system should also respect applicable recording and privacy laws.

  

Data Minimization

The system should retain the minimum information necessary for the intended purpose.

Potential lifecycle:

Audio → Transcription → Relevant Experience → Structured Insight → Pattern → Long-Term Knowledge

Raw recordings do not necessarily need to remain forever.

Different classes of information can have different retention periods.

  

Observation vs. Interpretation

The system must distinguish between:

Observable evidence

and

interpretation.

For example:

Observation:

“The person stopped responding and looked away.”

Interpretation:

“They were angry with me.”

The second statement should never automatically become fact.

The intelligence layer should preserve uncertainty and consider competing explanations.

  

Choice-Point Engine

The system identifies moments where:

Something happens → multiple responses become possible → a choice is made → the choice influences what happens next.

This becomes one of the fundamental units of personal intelligence.

Examples:

- Someone becomes quiet.
- Someone disagrees.
- A task becomes difficult.
- A relationship becomes strained.
- An opportunity appears.
- Fear or uncertainty emerges.
- I notice a familiar pattern.
- I choose whether to repeat or change a behavior.

Personal Intelligence Layer

The intelligence system looks across accumulated experience to identify:

- Recurring patterns
- Triggers
- Choice points
- Assumptions
- Alternative explanations
- Successful responses
- Failed responses
- Exceptions
- Relationships between goals and outcomes
- Emerging changes over time

The system should produce hypotheses rather than pretend to possess certainty.

  

Daily Reflection

Each morning:

Yesterday, you encountered three significant choice points.

For each one:

What happened

→ What you did

→ What you interpreted

→ What else might have been happening

→ What history suggests

→ What alternative response could be tested

The system then asks:

Would you like to experiment with a different response next time?

  

Weekly Pattern Review

The system periodically moves from individual events to patterns.

It asks:

What is becoming consistently true?

Possible outputs:

- Recurring triggers
- Recurring interpretations
- Recurring behavioral responses
- Recurring outcomes
- Conditions associated with better outcomes
- Conditions associated with worse outcomes
- Situations where my interpretation was repeatedly inaccurate
- Situations where a behavioral change appears to help

  

Personal Model

Over time, the system develops an evolving model of:

Goals  
What I am trying to accomplish.

Patterns  
What repeatedly happens.

Triggers  
What tends to precede those patterns.

Interpretations  
How I tend to understand situations.

Choices  
How I tend to respond.

Outcomes  
What tends to happen afterward.

Successful Alternatives  
What appears to work better.

Uncertainty  
What remains unknown.

The model must remain revisable.

It should never become:

“This is who you are.”

It should remain:

“This is our current best model based on the evidence available.”

  

From Personal Intelligence to Collective Intelligence

Once a personal pattern has been sufficiently validated, a new layer becomes possible.

The system asks:

Can this insight safely become useful beyond the individual?

Not everything should.

Some information remains permanently private.

Other information can be transformed into an anonymized pattern.

For example:

Personal Experience

Person A experienced unexpected tension during an ordinary conversation.

↓

Structured Pattern

In a subset of conversations, perceived withdrawal following repeated explanatory responses was associated with increased conversational tension.

↓

Anonymization

Remove or protect:

- Identity
- Exact location
- Identifying participants
- Raw recordings
- Unnecessary timestamps
- Sensitive details
- Relationship-specific information

↓

Collective Pattern

Pattern #4827:  
Similar conversational dynamics have appeared across multiple independent participants.

↓

Validation

Test whether the pattern:

- Reappears across different people
- Appears in different contexts
- Has meaningful exceptions
- Survives alternative explanations
- Produces reproducible outcomes

Only then should it become part of the collective knowledge layer.

  

The Pattern Commons

Validated anonymized patterns can become part of a shared Pattern Commons.

The Pattern Commons does not need to know:

“Jared experienced this.”

It only needs to know:

“This pattern has appeared under these conditions, with this degree of confidence.”

The individual disappears.

The useful pattern remains.

This transforms individual experience into collective learning without requiring the collective system to know everyone’s identity.

The Human GPS

The planetary mesh could eventually become something analogous to Waze.

Waze takes individual observations and turns them into a collective understanding of road conditions.

The Human GPS would take appropriately protected observations and turn them into a collective understanding of human conditions.

Instead of:

“There is traffic ahead.”

It might eventually be able to surface signals such as:

“People pursuing this goal tend to thrive in environments with these characteristics.”

Or:

“This type of interaction is associated with increased conflict under these conditions.”

Or:

“People with similar goals are gathering in these kinds of environments.”

Or:

“This community appears to be developing conditions associated with increased social connection.”

The system would not tell people what to do.

It would improve the information available for making their own choices.

Early Warning Signals

At sufficient scale, the planetary mesh could potentially detect patterns before they become obvious at the individual level.

For example:

Individual signals  
→ subtle changes

Multiple individuals  
→ recurring pattern

Groups  
→ increasing concentration

Communities  
→ emerging collective signal

Planetary Mesh  
→ early warning

The system could then surface:

“A pattern appears to be increasing.”

rather than waiting until:

“A problem has become obvious.”

This could apply to many domains, provided the data, models, and governance are sufficiently reliable.

Importantly, an early warning is not proof of causation.

The system should distinguish:

signal → hypothesis → investigation → evidence → confidence

  

Governance

As the system scales, governance becomes as important as technology.

The architecture should include:

- Individual ownership and control
- Explicit consent
- Revocable participation
- Data minimization
- Anonymization
- Transparent algorithms
- Auditability
- Ability to inspect why a pattern was surfaced
- Ability to challenge a conclusion
- Protection against discrimination
- Protection against coercive use
- Clear boundaries around what cannot be inferred or shared

The planetary mesh should never quietly become a system for ranking, controlling, or manipulating people.

Its purpose should remain:

Better information for better human choices.
