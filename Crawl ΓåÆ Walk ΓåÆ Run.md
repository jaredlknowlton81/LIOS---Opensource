Crawl → Walk → Run is a very good way to structure this.

🐛 Crawl — Learn to relate

Start entirely human and deliberately small.

- One group
- One shared context
- Simple communication/conflict protocols
- Capture what happened
- Reflect afterward
- Record agreements, tensions, decisions, and lessons

Goal: establish the behavioral and relational primitives.

Experience → Capture → Reflection → Repair

🚶 Walk — Give the system memory

Now introduce the Ledger + AI.

The system begins remembering:

- Who interacted with whom
- What happened
- What agreements existed
- Previous conflicts
- What interventions helped
- What patterns recur
- What changed afterward

AI becomes a sensemaking partner, not an authority.

Experience → Ledger → Sensemaking → Dialogue → Action → Feedback

Goal: demonstrate that persistent context improves collective learning

🏃 Run — Living Intelligence

Now scale across communities and environments.

Different groups can have their own local intelligence while contributing patterns to a larger mesh.

People ↔ Communities ↔ Agents ↔ AI ↔ Knowledge ↔ Environment

The system can begin identifying:

- recurring relational patterns
- successful conflict-resolution strategies
- emerging norms
- knowledge transfer between communities
- changes over time
- opportunities for intervention

And eventually:

Local intelligence → Network intelligence → Emergent intelligence

The deeper framing

This gives us a potentially powerful development path:

CRAWL: Can we help people relate better?  
↓  
WALK: Can we help a community remember and learn from its relationships?  
↓  
RUN: Can networks of communities learn from one another without losing their local context?

And that makes the Terenity material particularly interesting: it could be part of the Crawl layer—the human protocol from which the technological architecture grows.

The mistake would be starting at Run.

You earn planetary intelligence by first learning how two people repair a relationship
