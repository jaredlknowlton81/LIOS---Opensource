
☐ 1. Starting Point:

Finders Keypers

We began with the idea that Ministry of Lost Things: Finders Keypers is more than a physical puzzle.

It demonstrates a system where:

- ☐ Clues are distributed across physical artifacts
- ☐ Different envelopes contain different states of the experience
- ☐ Earlier discoveries provide context for later discoveries
- ☐ Physical objects interact with digital hints/instructions
- ☐ The player becomes the intelligence connecting the pieces
- ☐ The meaning of a clue can change as additional information appears
- ☐ The overall experience is sequential but potentially nonlinear in interpretation

Core observation

The intelligence isn’t contained in any individual clue. It emerges from the relationships among clues.

☐ 15. The Most Important Design Principle

We’ve effectively arrived at this:

Don’t build a puzzle where the clues have fixed meanings. Build an environment where meaning emerges from relationships.

That changes everything.

A key isn’t merely a key.

A card isn’t merely a card.

A cipher isn’t merely a cipher.

A word like LYOS isn’t necessarily something to decode.

Each becomes a node in a living relational system.

☐ 17. The Deepest Question

The conversation ultimately moved beyond puzzles.

The question became:

Can intelligence emerge from agents interacting with a persistent, relational, evolving information environment?

If yes, then the architecture isn’t fundamentally about storing information.

It’s about preserving relationships through time so that new intelligence can emerge from them.

And the physical/digital/federated puzzle becomes a demonstration model of that principle.

In one sentence:

We are imagining a living system in which physical and digital experiences continuously create relationships, those relationships persist in a shared context layer, AI makes sense of them, and the resulting intelligence feeds back into the world—changing what happens next.
