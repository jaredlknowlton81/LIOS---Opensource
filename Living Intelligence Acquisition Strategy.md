Purpose

The purpose of acquiring technology is not simply to obtain better devices.

The goal is to progressively build and test the architecture of Living Intelligence, beginning with lived personal experience and eventually extending toward:

[[PAI]] → [[Fieldy]] → [[Relational Intelligence]] → [[Community Intelligence]] → [[Network Intelligence]] → [[Planetary Intelligence]]

The guiding principle is:

Acquire the capability that strengthens the weakest part of the intelligence loop, using the lowest-risk mechanism that allows the experiment to proceed.

The objective is therefore not technological accumulation.

It is capability acquisition in service of experimentation.

Acquisition Is Not the Same as Ownership

A central principle is:

The objective is to acquire access to capability, not necessarily to own the underlying asset.

A capability may be acquired through:

- existing resources
- direct purchase
- used / refurbished equipment
- rental
- leasing
- borrowing
- institutional access
- community access
- sponsorship
- manufacturer pilot
- research partnership
- fellowship
- stipend
- grant
- shared infrastructure
- eventual development / construction

Ownership should therefore be treated as one acquisition mechanism among many.

Acquisition Decision Framework

Before acquiring anything, ask:

1. What capability is missing?

What part of the Living Intelligence loop is currently weakest?

2. What infrastructure does it strengthen?

Does it improve:

- [[Energy]]
- [[Communications]]
- [[Transportation]]
- [[Finance]]
- [[Knowledge]]
- [[AI]]
- [[Ledger]]

3. What hypothesis does it test?

A purchase should ideally enable a concrete experiment.

4. Can the capability already be accessed?

Before purchasing, consider:

- existing devices
- borrowing
- institutional access
- community resources
- partnerships
- rentals
- leases

5. Can someone else fund the experiment?

Consider:

- grants
- fellowships
- stipends
- sponsorship
- research partnerships
- manufacturer pilots

6. What is the minimum viable acquisition?

Acquire the smallest capability that can test the hypothesis.

7. What would justify scaling?

Define the evidence required before:

- purchasing permanently
- upgrading
- expanding
- deploying to others
- building infrastructure

Core Acquisition Principle

Do not buy technology because it is better technology.

  

Acquire capability because the architecture has demonstrated a need for it.

And:

Prefer access over ownership when uncertainty is high.

And:

Prefer experiments over accumulation.

And:

Prefer externally funded experiments when the experiment produces knowledge that can benefit others.

And ultimately:

Let learning determine what we acquire next.

Master Principle

The goal is not to accumulate technology.

  

The goal is to progressively build the smallest system capable of testing the next hypothesis about Living Intelligence.

The architecture should determine the experiment.

The experiment should determine the capability.

The capability should determine the acquisition mechanism.

The acquisition should generate experience.

Experience should enter the Ledger.

The Ledger should enable learning.

Learning should determine what comes next.

That is the acquisition loop of Living Intelligence.
